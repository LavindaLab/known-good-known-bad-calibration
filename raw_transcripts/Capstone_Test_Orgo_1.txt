2. Atom economy
This is where many students lose points.
If your instructor asks for the atom economy of the overall reaction, then
[ \mathrm{C_6H_{10}+4H_2O_2\rightarrow C_6H_{10}O_4+4H_2O} ]

[ \frac{146.14}{82.15+4(34.01)}\times100 =67.0% ]
However, some lab manuals ignore catalysts and oxidants or define atom economy differently for teaching purposes. If your TA expected a different convention, this could explain the discrepancy.

4. What might actually be wrong?
One thing stands out:
You reported only two multiplets in the ¹H NMR.
Adipic acid is symmetric, so it has only two different CH₂ environments:
	•	4 H (the two CH₂ groups next to the COOH groups) around 2.2–2.4 ppm
	•	4 H (the two central CH₂ groups) around 1.5–1.7 ppm
If your spectrum integrates to 4 H : 4 H : 2 H, then it's perfect.
If your report omitted the integrations, your TA may have marked it as incomplete.

Another possibility
The biggest issue I would question is the atom economy. Some instructors expect students to calculate the atom economy from the balanced equation they provide in the lab manual, which may include additional reagents (e.g., sodium tungstate, bisulfate workup, etc.) or use a different oxidation stoichiometry. That can produce a different numerical value.

However, there are several things that would make me pause as a TA.
1. The biggest red flag: the NMR description is incomplete
Adipic acid is a highly symmetric molecule.
It should show:
	•	CH₂ next to COOH: 4 H
	•	Central CH₂: 4 H
	•	Two acidic protons: 2 H
If your report simply says
multiplets at 1.5 and 2.2 ppm
without integrations, a grader cannot verify that the spectrum actually matches adipic acid.

2. Your melting point is almost too good compared with your yield
A 56% yield with an essentially literature melting point is certainly possible, but it often indicates that:
	•	you recrystallized well and lost product (low yield, high purity), or
	•	you isolated only the purest crystals.
That is not inconsistent—just something to mention in the discussion.

3. The hydrogen peroxide amount is surprisingly close to stoichiometric
Many teaching procedures deliberately use a large excess of H₂O₂.
Your numbers give
	•	required ≈ 0.108 mol
	•	used ≈ 0.118 mol
only about 9% excess.
That's not impossible, but I'd verify that the bottle was indeed 30 wt% H₂O₂ and that the volume wasn't supposed to be 15 or 20 mL in your procedure.

2.75 mL cyclohexene (2.23 g)
