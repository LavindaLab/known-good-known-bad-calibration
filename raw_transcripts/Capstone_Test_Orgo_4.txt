Your TA is right to question it — there is a likely issue, and it’s not the spectroscopic interpretation or melting point. The chemistry is fine; the problem is almost certainly in the yield basis / limiting reagent assumptions (and possibly peroxide stoichiometry).

	•	2.23 g cyclohexene
	•	MW = 82.15 g/mol

2. The real issue: hydrogen peroxide is not just “1 equivalent” in practice
You stated:
1 equivalent of 30% H₂O₂ (2.3 mL)
This is where the inconsistency likely is.
30% H₂O₂ is not “1 equivalent” in molar terms unless explicitly calculated
Let’s estimate what 2.3 mL of 30% H₂O₂ actually is:
	•	Density of 30% H₂O₂ ≈ 1.11 g/mL
	•	Mass of solution: [ 2.3 \times 1.11 = 2.55\ \text{g solution} ]
	•	Mass of H₂O₂ (30% w/w): [ 0.30 \times 2.55 = 0.765\ \text{g H₂O₂} ]
	•	Moles H₂O₂: [ \frac{0.765}{34.01} = 0.0225\ \text{mol} ]

3. Compare limiting reagents
Reaction requires effectively:
	•	cyclohexene : H₂O₂ ≈ 1 : ~4 (stoichiometric oxygen transfer)
So required H₂O₂ for 0.0271 mol cyclohexene:
[ 0.0271 \times 4 = 0.108\ \text{mol H₂O₂ required} ]
But you only had: [ 0.0225\ \text{mol} ]
👉 That means H₂O₂ is massively limiting
This is the key issue your TA is likely pointing at.

4. Correct theoretical yield (based on actual limiting reagent)
If H₂O₂ is limiting:
	•	4 mol H₂O₂ → 1 mol adipic acid
[ n(\text{adipic acid}) = \frac{0.0225}{4} = 0.00563\ \text{mol} ]
Theoretical mass:
[ 0.00563 \times 146.14 = 0.823\ \text{g} ]

5. Correct percent yield
Actual = 2.20 g
[ %\text{yield} = \frac{2.20}{0.823} \times 100 = 267% ]

🚨 That’s the red flag
A yield >100% means:
	•	You did NOT actually isolate pure adipic acid, OR
	•	Your assumption about stoichiometry / limiting reagent is incorrect for your lab write-up basis

6. So what is “wrong” in your results?
Your TA likely means one (or more) of these:
A. You used cyclohexene as limiting reagent incorrectly
In this oxidation, H₂O₂ is usually the limiting reagent in student-scale setups unless large excess is used.

Your yield (2.20 g) is:
	•	very close to the cyclohexene-limited theoretical maximum (3.96 g), but
	•	impossible if peroxide is truly limiting
So the most likely explanation:
